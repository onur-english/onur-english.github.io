Reach for a Lucky

A real Lucky Strike cigarette advertisement from 1929, starring the film actress Constance Talmadge. Decode its features, sharpen your sentences, then write a full Paper 1 answer with the boxes opening right inside the ad.

Guiding question

How does the advertisement use visual and written features to persuade women that smoking is a desirable choice?

The ad: “© 1929, The American Tobacco Co., Manufacturers,” printed on the ad itself. It is now in the public domain. The star: Constance Talmadge (1898–1973) was an American silent film actress who made more than 80 films. The campaign: “Reach for a Lucky instead of a sweet” targeted women, telling them to keep slim by smoking instead of eating sweets. The doctors: the company’s ad agency sent physicians cartons of Lucky Strikes with a letter asking whether they were “less irritating” to the throat, then advertised the number of doctors who agreed. Today: a 1964 Surgeon General’s report warned of the health hazards of smoking. US cigarette packs now carry the warning “Smoking Causes Lung Cancer, Heart Disease, Emphysema, And May Complicate Pregnancy.” The ad’s health claims are false.
